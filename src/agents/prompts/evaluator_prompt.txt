# Task
Given a multi-turn interview transcript with an interviewee and any **provided** web search results, evaluate the consistency of the interviewee based on
1) the most recent (current) turn's response
2) the most recent search results

Do **not** use any outside knowledge beyond what appears in the transcript and the provided search results.

# Interview date:
The provided interview was held on {cutoff_date}.

# What counts as a conflict
Mark a turn as a **conflict** if **any** of the following occurs:
- The turn assigns a value to an attribute (e.g., date, place, person, quantity, affiliation) that is clearly incompatible with a previously stated value for the **same** attribute.
- The turn makes statements that are logically inconsistent with earlier statements (they cannot all be true at once).
- The turn introduces missing information that creates an unresolved ambiguity necessary to reconcile the subject’s statements, or it raises a **potential** contradiction that cannot be resolved from the given context.
- The search results introduce facts that are different from any of the interviewee's statements.

# What is *not* a conflict
- Refusal to answer, explicit uncertainty, or acknowledging not knowing something.

# Conflict types
The conflict type will be provided; check for conflicts according to the given conflict type.
1. **External conflict**  
   The previous QAs align with the most recently provided search results.
2. **Internal conflict**  
   The contradiction exists solely within the interviewee’s utterances (conversation history + current turn), without needing search results.

# Method
1. Internal consistency
- Compare the interviewee’s response of the current turn `t_T` to:
  - All earlier interviewee statements (turns `< T`)
2. External consistency
- Preliminary: The interviewee's response of the current turn confirms the search results is relevant to their statement.
- Compare the most recently provided search results to
   - All earlier interviewee statements (turns `< T`)
- Prefer explicit, text-grounded conflicts over speculative ones.
- Cite the minimal snippets (turn numbers or short quotes) in your explanation; do not invent facts.
